# Kytaiv Ponds


::: {.flatplan_section .flatplan_layout_left_text .flatplan_flow_false}

::: {.flatplan_story_text .flatplan_width_narrow}
Down in the wooded hollow, the air thickens. Here, the forest steps straight into black, stagnant water—a liminal space where dry land, water, and decay blur into one another, forming a quiet, dark pastoral. The place holds a heavy, almost oppressive stillness that is hard to name, and its spirit keeps its distance. Tangled reflections, duckweed, and dense gloom swallow the light, resisting any attempt to translate that viscous, haunting weight into a flat frame.

Every now and then, the water settles just enough for the chaos to lock into a strange geometry. Drowned timber and mirrored branches align, turning the dark surface into an abstract grid where up and down lose their meaning. In those rare moments, the landscape stops being a mere forest pond and reveals a quiet, melancholic grace of its own.

A walk begins at dawn—a couple of hours circling the water while the hollow still traps the morning shadow. Once the sun climbs high enough to reach the surface, the spell breaks, and if you are lucky, you leave with a frame or two.
